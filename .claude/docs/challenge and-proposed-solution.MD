# Our Product Vision

Instead of focusing only on dashboards, our team wants to solve an earlier problem in the educational journey.

We believe universities do not lack dashboards.

They lack engagement.

The biggest opportunity is increasing platform usage.

Higher usage naturally generates richer educational analytics.

Therefore our solution focuses on increasing professor engagement first.

---

# Problem We Identified

One member of our team previously worked as a university professor.

According to his experience, one of the largest pains professors face is preparing lectures.

Creating high-quality educational material takes a considerable amount of time.

Because of this:

- Professors prepare fewer classes.
- Students receive fewer guided learning experiences.
- Students use the platform less frequently.
- Universities perceive less value.
- Engagement decreases.
- Churn risk increases.

Patient360 currently behaves as a passive educational repository.

Our proposal is transforming it into an active AI teaching assistant.

---

# Product Vision

Patient360 becomes an AI-powered teaching assistant.

Instead of manually preparing classes, professors simply describe the lecture they want.

Example:

> "Prepare a two-hour lecture about Prostate Cancer."

The platform automatically generates:

- Lecture outline
- Teaching objectives
- Slide deck
- Speaker notes
- Learning sequence
- Suggested clinical cases
- Homework recommendations
- Additional educational resources

This dramatically reduces lecture preparation time.

---

# Product Philosophy

Instead of asking:

"What clinical case should I use?"

The professor asks:

"Create tomorrow's lecture."

Patient360 becomes part of the professor's daily workflow.

The platform shifts from being content storage to becoming an educational assistant.

# MVP Scope

The production platform currently contains more than 400 clinical cases.

However, implementing semantic search over the entire dataset is unnecessary for the hackathon MVP.

To maximize feasibility and implementation speed, the MVP will intentionally reduce scope.

Instead of indexing every clinical case, we will manually curate approximately **five representative clinical cases**.

These cases will cover different medical specialties and demonstrate the recommendation engine.

The architecture, however, must be designed to scale naturally from 5 cases to 400+ cases without requiring significant changes.

# AI Architecture

The application will NOT train a custom language model.

Training is unnecessary.

Instead, the system will use Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

Architecture:

Professor Request

↓

LLM understands lecture topic

↓

Semantic Search

↓

Relevant clinical cases

↓

LLM generates lecture

↓

Slides include recommended Patient360 cases

This architecture is significantly cheaper, simpler and more scalable.

---

# Why Semantic Search?

Clinical terminology varies considerably.

A professor may write:

Chest pain

Another may write:

Acute Coronary Syndrome

Another:

Myocardial Infarction

Keyword search performs poorly.

Semantic Search compares meaning instead of exact words.

This dramatically improves recommendation quality.

---

# Why RAG Instead of Fine-Tuning?

Fine-tuning would require:

Training datasets

GPU infrastructure

Retraining

High cost

Long implementation time

Not feasible for a hackathon.

RAG offers:

Very low cost

Easy implementation

Fast iteration

Scalability

Excellent recommendation quality
